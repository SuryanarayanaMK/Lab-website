Fall 2026: EN.553.413/613 — Applied Statistics and Data Science. An applied introduction to probability, statistics, and data science centered on a fundamental question: how can we learn reliably from finite, noisy data? The course develops probability and random variables as the mathematical language of uncertainty, then builds toward estimation, sampling distributions, confidence intervals, hypothesis testing, and resampling methods. These ideas are then developed through statistical modeling, from linear and generalized linear models to model assessment, selection, regularization, and gentle introduction to nonlinear regression. Throughout the course, we emphasize the connections between classical statistical inference and modern data science, including prediction versus inference, overfitting, generalization, and multiple testing. Concepts are developed through real datasets and computational experiments, culminating in a modern workflow for reasoning about uncertainty, building models, evaluating their assumptions, and drawing defensible conclusions from data.
