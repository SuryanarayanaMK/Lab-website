Education

Teaching

Over the past decade, I have had the privilege of teaching a variety of courses at Johns Hopkins University, MIT, Stanford, and ETH Zurich — an opportunity to learn new subjects, often just a little before teaching them.

Undergraduate and graduate courses I have taught span engineering, computer science, and biology, and include topics such as Computational Biology, Machine Learning for Biomedical Data, Biophysical Modeling, Numerical Methods for PDEs, Systems Biology, and Physics-Informed Neural Networks.

I have also introduced several new courses to the curricula — including undergraduate and graduate courses on Computational Biology, Deep Learning for Science, Soft Matter Physics, and Quantitative Cell Biology. At Johns Hopkins, I have created Freshman Seminars on the Science of Everyday Life, Introduction to Biological Modeling, and Data Science for the Physical Sciences.

Away from my academic home, I have been a regular summer and winter school lecturer. At the Marine Biological Laboratory in Woods Hole, I was the Founding Co-director of their newest Summer School on Computational Biology. At the Santa Fe Institute, I was a lead instructor for the Complex Systems Summer School. I have also taught at the London Mathematical Society Summer School, the Les Houches school on Soft Matter, the Boulder school on condensed matter physics, and the KITP program on Active Matter.
